## Remaining limitations

The field is the supplied algorithm's spatial Gaussian construction; this review
adds clarity, not a new physically validated quantum solver. Layer scales are fixed
within one excerpt, not across different parameter runs. The pair risk index and
field strength remain distinct quantities. Original research models still have no
autonomous controller; O/S/Z are separate control examples.

Member avoidance could reduce external obstacle clearance; a follow-up joint
clearance layer addresses that interaction on the default fixtures (below), but
this review still does not establish collision freedom for arbitrary parameters or
initial overlap. Reference-route display and playback fixes do not change that
limitation. Cloud cold starts and computation/network latency remain;
loading feedback and compressed transport are retained. Browser progress is
session-local and is not persisted across a full browser refresh.

## Follow-up: joint clearance with external obstacles

The interaction above was reworked rather than left as-is. Three coupled causes
were identified in the coordinated O/S/Z adapters:

- The formation clock was frozen while a partner pair was active. When it resumed,
the ships carried a large catch-up demand and accelerated through whatever
external trigger domain they were crossing.
- The speed PID kept integrating while the ship was deflecting around an external
obstacle, so it left the trigger domain and then accelerated back through it.
- Pair detection planned exactly to the requested gap, and the bounded 1.5 m/s²
braking delay then finished 0.16 m below it on the circle route.

Repairs, all inside the coordinated mode only (member avoidance set to zero still
restores unmodified source-style speed updates):

- The formation clock keeps running while a pair is active; yield headings and
speed caps resolve the pair.
- The speed integral is held/decayed while external avoidance is active and is
released afterwards.
- Pair detection plans against one extra metre of separation.
- Adapter version is now `control-adapter-3`.

Default coordinated runs, metres:

| Route | External minimum, before | External minimum, after | Member minimum, after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| O | 6.568 | 14.190 | 18.608 |
| S | 6.411 | 10.874 | 39.788 |
| Z | 18.374 | 18.364 | 32.679 |

Uncoordinated baselines are 8.641 / 10.612 / 18.373 m, so the coordinated route is
no longer worse than switching coordination off on these fixtures. O's recorded
minimum equals the initial standoff, meaning no later approach is closer. A
30-case one-parameter sweep (avoidance strength 2.5–6, cruise 3–5 m/s, expected
separation 14–24 m, static trigger radius 25–40 m) improved 12 cases, left 13
within 0.2 m and lowered 5; four of the five lowered cases were already inside a
10 m centre distance without coordination. The controller remains a heuristic
bang-bang deflection whose near passes are sensitive to encounter phase, so this
is a mitigation measured on fixtures, not a collision-free guarantee, and arbitrary
initial states or parameter extremes remain unproven.
